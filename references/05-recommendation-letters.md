# 05 — Recommendation Letters / Referee Authenticity

## Core principle
A recommendation letter is a third party’s **evidence-based judgment**, not a personal statement in the professor’s voice. Drafts may be prepared for referee review, but the referee must freely verify, edit, approve and submit their own evaluation. Do not invent identity, contact data, relationship length, praise, ranking, observations or signature.

## First collect referee-specific evidence
- Official role, institution and relation (course teacher, research supervisor, internship line manager); when/how long observed.
- Course/project name and scope, applicant’s actual work, assignments and grade only if verified.
- **Two or three scenes witnessed by recommender**, ideally a question they raised, the applicant’s response, measurable output, behavior on iteration, or observed intellectual independence.
- Peer comparison only if the recommender can substantiate it (not made-up top 1%).
- Programme or capability targeted; boundaries to claims (ongoing research vs completed finding).
- Explicit sign-off from referee.

## Typical 4–5 paragraph progression
1. Identity, basis of relationship, supported overall assessment.
2. Specific academic/research/technical example showing approach to reasoning.
3. Another distinct instance: response to critique, collaborative habit, initiative, communication, work ethic.
4. Calibrated comparison, future readiness and constructive specificity.
5. Decisive but proportionate closing, with true referee contact/signature details added by referee.

## Two letters must be distinct
Do NOT write two generic letters listing the same four virtues. Divide evidence: lecturer A can discuss model reasoning and intellectual curiosity; lecturer B can discuss practical financial statement interpretation or responsibility; research mentor can evaluate hypothesis-to-test design and alternative mechanisms. Each letter’s tone and observations should sound as though only that person could have written it.

## Research RA nuance
A research project with a conceptual framework is not a published causal finding. For a GenAI/political-capital style project, an RA might distinguish `overall workforce contraction` from `job composition reallocation`, propose what data would be needed, and consider government-contract vs labor-reorganization mechanisms. This shows intellectual care, not proof that either channel exists. Explicitly separate proposed tests, implemented tests, and results.

## Course lecturer nuance
Specific classroom evidence beats adjectives: applicant compares multi-year turnover indicators against a peer, explains accounting definitions, and looks beyond ratio movements to operating context. The professor may only attest to what they genuinely witnessed (assignment, presentation, office hour). Avoid claiming access to internship performance unless directly known.

## Sample phrase boundaries
Weak: `She is brilliant, extremely hardworking, and among the finest students I have ever taught.`
Better when supported: `Her analysis did not stop at the movement in the ratios; she checked whether differences in operating context could explain the comparison.`
Neither sentence may be used as an actual referee claim without that referee’s confirmation.

## QA
Check recommender identity, relationships and dates; course/project naming; evidence witnessed; confidentiality; letterhead requirements; file format; avoidance of cloned prose across letters. Never forge or send as the recommender. See [templates/rl-evidence.md](../templates/rl-evidence.md).
