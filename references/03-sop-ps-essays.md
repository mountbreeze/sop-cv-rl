# 03 — SOP, Personal Statement & Essay Writing

## Principle: one narrative spine
Main line usually: industry/problem context → applicant’s encounter with a particular problem → method and view refined in academic/work evidence → genuine skill gap → programme mechanism → concrete professional action. For short answers, select just the relevant parts. Every paragraph must advance the reasoning, not separately announce “I am passionate”.

Do not translate CV bullets into prose. A CV says **what**; essay explains **why this changed judgment** and **what new analytical question remains**.

## Opening
Avoid `My interest in finance began with…`, `I have always been passionate…`, `One question has always fascinated me…`, `Since childhood…`, and ritual `During a project, I first realised…`. Options:
- A concrete disagreement between two interpretations of business evidence.
- A real academic puzzle/methodological limitation.
- An industry transition, stated briefly, returning immediately to applicant actions.
Avoid turning the opening into an industry report or namedropping consulting firm research.

## Evidence-rich body
Internship paragraphs: actual assignment → why initial metric/method was insufficient → narrower reframe → actions taken → defensible output → how method developed. An important decision can be “what evidence would justify a forecast assumption” rather than “I led a billion-dollar deal”.

Academic paragraphs must cite concrete reasoning: a coursework exercise, model assumption, accounting classification, financial econometrics interpretation or research method; explain what intuition was challenged and how it connects to the intended programme. **GPA and course names alone do not show academic purpose.** Do not describe fabricated professor conversations.

## Why this programme
At most 2–3 **verified, current** and genuinely relevant resources in most essays. For each: applicant’s problem → missing conceptual/methodological capacity → precise programme resource → proposed exercise/engagement → later use. Explain an engineering ecosystem, research seminar, consulting project, internship opportunity etc only when officially offered and relevant. Don't write `Course A teaches X; course B teaches Y`.

## Career goals
Distinguish near-term role (specific function, skills and stakeholders), longer-term decision responsibility and practical barriers. Explain any IB → buy-side or research → policy/industry transition. Replace `make an impact` by decisions such as improving how growth assumptions translate into cash-flow scenarios, rigor in credit analysis, or verified deployment strategy. No unsupported guaranteed roles or offers.

## Community / diversity
Use a concrete collaborative behavior: understand others’ frame, identify evidence gaps, share market knowledge in a named **verified** discussion environment, contribute to peer projects. Network value comes from sustained exchange, not “collecting connections”.

## Separate prompts and limits
A segmented multi-question writing sample is not one continuous PS. Place the **exact question as clear title** and answer it independently, in the required order. Never reuse stock opening across questions. Specific prompt always outranks generic template.

For explicit word caps, aim close without crossing: 500→470–500; 400→380–400; 300→285–300; 250→235–250; 200→190–200; 150→140–150. Historical reference sometimes tolerates 200→210 or 150→155, **but a hard maximum cannot be exceeded.** General PS with no cap often ~1000–1200 words; exact prompt overrides. Character limits (e.g., 4000 **characters**) are not word limits: count spaces according to application rules, and maintain safety buffer.

## Editing sequence
Pass 1 substantive arc; Pass 2 programme-specific intellectual link; Pass 3 fact verification; Pass 4 sentence-level AI-style audit; Pass 5 count and question compliance. Deleting repetition beats adding generic closings. After draft, show 3–5 most consequential edits and remaining questions when asked.

## AI-style audit: replace instead of cosmetically polishing
Avoid repeated `This experience taught me / made me realise / I learned`. Avoid repeated rhetorical questions and “One question bothered me”. Avoid ornamental triads (three abstract nouns/verbs), `transformative`, `profound`, `unique opportunity`, `world-class`, `holistic`, `dynamic landscape`, `broaden my horizons`, `perfect fit`. Avoid artificial `Another experience / Similarly` transitions. Prefer objective problem and methodological changes.

Example bad: `I improved research, communication, and leadership skills.`
Better: `I reorganized the analysis around the decisions it needed to support, leaving assumptions without a verifiable source in the sensitivity cases.`
This is a style illustration, not an applicant fact.

## Topic-specific tone
- Oxford/LSE academic-intensive programmes: foreground intellectual concern, academic coursework methods and fit. If user says “reduce internships,” do not reflexively insert internship examples in opener. The back half can give career plans + actions during programme + *why school makes those actions more powerful*.
- Applied Hong Kong/Singapore finance and management programmes: connect evidenced responsibilities with immediate capability gaps and operational career steps. Avoid interchangeable university paragraphs.
- AI/analytics: what the model/data can and cannot establish, and implementation constraints; avoid pretending business applicant is an ML research scientist.
- Scholarship/challenge essay: answer the precise evaluation dimension rather than recycling “why school”.
